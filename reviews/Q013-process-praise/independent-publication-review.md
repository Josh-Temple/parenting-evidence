## Independent bottom line

A balanced conclusion is:

> 固定的な能力・人格ラベルより、実際の工夫、strategy、取り組み方、行動に焦点を当てるfeedbackを支持する研究はある。ただし、process praiseが常にperson praiseより優れるわけではなく、幼児ではperson / product / process praiseがいずれもneutral feedbackよりmotivationを高めた実験もある。praiseのtiming、sincerity、実際の行動とのfitが重要で、process praiseも多ければ多いほどよいわけではない。長期のparent-praise evidenceは小規模な一つのcohortへの依存が大きく、academic achievementへの因果効果は確立していない。

This supports cautious preference for specific/action/strategy-focused feedback, not a universal “effort praise” script.

## Claim reconstruction

### C001 — process praise is always better than person praise

Decision: NOT ESTABLISHED AS A UNIVERSAL RULE

Kamins & Dweck 1999 supports process > person after setback in one preschool role-play paradigm.

But:
- Henderlong Corpus & Lepper 2007 found person / product / process praise all improved motivation relative to neutral feedback in preschool children
- Bennett-Pierre 2024 found no preregistered primary process-vs-person difference in older children
- Xu 2024 suggests nonlinear praise-balance associations rather than a monotonic process-praise advantage

Therefore the universal claim fails.

### C002 — person praise can increase vulnerability after setbacks

Decision: SUPPORTED IN SOME EXPERIMENTAL CONTEXTS

Kamins & Dweck 1999 directly supports more helpless responses after person praise in 5–6-year-olds.

Zentall & Morris 2010 provides related evidence around generic vs nongeneric praise and post-failure motivation.

Boundary:
These are short experimental/scenario outcomes, not proof that occasional “頭がいいね” causes durable harm.

### C003 — toddler process praise predicts later incremental frameworks

Decision: SUPPORTED AS AN ASSOCIATION

Gunderson 2013:
- N=53
- naturalistic praise at 14 / 26 / 38 months
- process praise predicted later incremental motivational frameworks

Boundary:
Observational prediction is not causal intervention evidence.

### C004 — process praise means “praise effort regardless of context”

Decision: FALSE / TOO BROAD

Process praise includes:
- effort
- strategy
- persistence
- specific action

Radovanovic 2023 shows that timing and alignment with child action matter.

Therefore “say 頑張ったね frequently” is not equivalent to evidence-based process feedback.

### C005 — more process praise is always better

Decision: NOT ESTABLISHED

Radovanovic 2023:
- randomly dispersed / high-volume process praise could relate negatively to persistence

Xu 2024:
- inverted-U relationship between process-to-person balance and mastery motivation

The evidence directly argues against a simple monotonic “more is better” rule.

### C006 — process praise improves later academic achievement

Decision: ASSOCIATION / MEDIATION; CAUSAL EFFECT NOT ISOLATED

Gunderson 2018 uses the **same N=53 cohort** as Gunderson 2013.

This is a longitudinal predictive mediation result, not a second independent replication and not a randomized parenting intervention.

### C007 — all praise is equally reinforcing

Decision: NOT ESTABLISHED

Senn 2020 shows strong individual variability in praise as a behavioral reinforcer.

This outcome domain is distinct from mindset/persistence but is useful against universal reinforcement assumptions.

### C008 — one practical wording is universally optimal

Decision: NOT ESTABLISHED

Evidence varies by:
- child age
- task
- praise target
- timing
- sincerity
- amount
- outcome measured

No universal phrase or ratio is established.

## Required challenge 1

Question:
**Would a cautious preference for specific/process-focused feedback survive if the Dweck/Gunderson research line were removed?**

Decision:
**YES, but the claim becomes narrower.**

Without that research line:
- Henderlong & Lepper 2002 still supports controllable, sincere, autonomy-supportive praise
- Radovanovic 2023 supports timing/alignment
- Xu 2024 argues against extreme praise balance
- Senn 2020 supports individual variability
- Henderlong Corpus & Lepper 2007 prevents a simple “person praise is bad” conclusion

The remaining evidence supports context-sensitive, specific feedback more than a strict process-vs-person rule.

## Required challenge 2

Question:
**Does the long-term evidence represent multiple independent replications?**

Decision:
**NO.**

Gunderson 2013 and 2018 are the same N=53 cohort.

The current Review explicitly avoids double-counting them.

## Required challenge 3

Question:
**Could the Reader Layer be interpreted as “praise effort as much as possible”?**

Decision:
**NO after current wording.**

The Review explicitly states:
- process praise includes strategy/action
- timing and fit matter
- high-volume/dispersed process praise may not help
- recent preschool evidence is nonlinear

## Required challenge 4

Question:
**Does Japan-specific evidence directly confirm process > person praise?**

Decision:
**NO.**

Aoki 2005 is useful context on age/wording differences in Japanese children but does not test the Q013 process-vs-person causal contrast.

The Japan-facing evidence limitation remains explicit.
