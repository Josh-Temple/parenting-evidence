## 1. Henderlong & Lepper 2002

Sources:
- PMID 12206194
- DOI 10.1037/0033-2909.128.5.774

Verified:
- praise can enhance, reduce, or have no effect on intrinsic motivation depending on context
- sincerity, controllable attribution, autonomy, competence information, social comparison and attainable standards are material moderators

Interpretation:
PASS.
The Review correctly avoids treating praise as a single uniform reinforcer or process praise as a universal script.

## 2. Kamins & Dweck 1999

Sources:
- PMID 10380873
- DOI 10.1037/0012-1649.35.3.835

Verified:
- praise study N=64
- age 5–6
- person / outcome / process praise
- subsequent setback measures included self-assessment, affect and persistence
- person praise produced more helpless responses than process praise

Interpretation:
PASS_WITH_LAB_BOUNDARY.
Direct preschool experimental evidence supports vulnerability after person praise in this paradigm, but the role-play study is small and short-term.

## 3. Henderlong Corpus & Lepper 2007

Source:
- DOI 10.1080/01443410601159852

Verified:
- preschool study N=76
- person / product / process praise vs neutral feedback
- all three praise conditions enhanced motivation relative to neutral feedback in preschool children

Interpretation:
PASS.
This is an important counterexample to the claim that process praise is always superior to person praise in preschoolers.

## 4. Gunderson et al. 2013 / 2018

Sources:
- PMID 23397904
- PMID 29172567

Verified:
- naturalistic parent-child praise observed at 14 / 26 / 38 months
- N=53
- more process praise predicted more incremental motivational frameworks at age 7–8
- early person praise did not map to later fixed frameworks in the same simple way
- 2018 fourth-grade achievement analysis follows the **same N=53 cohort**
- later academic outcomes were statistically mediated through incremental trait beliefs

Interpretation:
PASS_WITH_DEPENDENCE_BOUNDARY.
2013 and 2018 are not independent replications. Long-term achievement evidence remains predictive/mediational, not causal proof that changing praise wording will raise achievement.

## 5. Zentall & Morris 2010

Sources:
- PMID 20570281
- DOI 10.1016/j.jecp.2010.04.015

Verified:
- N=135 kindergarteners
- praise consistency / generic-nongeneric mix was manipulated
- more nongeneric praise related linearly to greater motivation
- persistence and self-evaluation responded differently

Interpretation:
PASS.
Do not create a rigid household praise ratio from this scenario experiment.

## 6. Radovanovic et al. 2023

Sources:
- PMID 37410443
- DOI 10.1037/dev0001584

Verified:
- Experiment 1: N=29, age 17–18 months
- Experiment 2: N=60, age 17–31 months
- praise temporally aligned with trying + success related to greater persistence
- effect was more robust for process than general praise
- high-volume / randomly dispersed process praise that did not correspond to child action related negatively to persistence

Interpretation:
PASS.
Timing and fit matter; “more process praise is always better” is not supported.

## 7. Senn et al. 2020

Sources:
- PMID 31219190
- DOI 10.1002/jaba.591

Verified:
- praise alone functioned as an effective reinforcer for 2 of 6 preschoolers in the initial assessment
- remaining participants required additional reinforcers
- descriptive vs general praise did not yield one universal pattern

Interpretation:
PASS_WITH_OUTCOME_BOUNDARY.
Behavioral reinforcement is distinct from post-failure motivation / mindset.

## 8. Xu et al. 2024

Source:
- DOI 10.1016/j.appdev.2023.101607

Verified:
- parents of 126 children age 4–6
- process-to-person praise balance based on parent report
- inverted-U associations with several mastery-motivation outcomes
- extreme process-heavy or person-heavy balance was not uniformly optimal

Interpretation:
PASS_WITH_NONCAUSAL_BOUNDARY.
Useful recent evidence against a monotonic “more process praise is better” rule, but not a randomized causal study and not a basis for a household ratio target.

## 9. Bennett-Pierre et al. 2024

Source:
- PMID 39111151

Verified:
- N=150, older than primary preschool scope
- preregistered primary analyses found no clear process-vs-person difference in persistence/self-evaluation
- exploratory comparison suggested process praise > control for persistence

Interpretation:
PASS as older-child countercheck only.
This supports context dependence, not a preschool null conclusion.

## 10. Japan boundary

Aoki 2005:
- Japanese preschool / Grade 1 study
- compared “ありがとう”, “上手”, and nodding in a helping context
- subsequent helping differed by age / feedback type

Interpretation:
Japan-specific evidence shows praise wording/context can matter, but it does **not** directly test process-vs-person praise.

Targeted search:
A strong Japanese parent process-vs-person randomized or longitudinal study in ages 0–6 was not identified in this pass.

Wording:
