## Interpretation by claim

### C001 — early childcare generally harms development

Not supported as a general claim.

The Japanese cohort shows favorable associations, several European quasi-experiments show benefits in some domains, and quality meta-analyses point toward small positive associations. Some systems / studies show behavioral disadvantages, so "no harm anywhere" would also be too strong.

### C002 — Japan-specific developmental association

Supported as an observational association.

The JECS result is large, prospective and directly relevant to Japan. The strong baseline differences between care users and nonusers prevent a clean causal interpretation.

### C003 — early childcare generally improves development

Not established causally as a universal claim.

The direction varies by outcome and system. Benefits may be stronger for disadvantaged children in some contexts.

### C004 — quality matters

Supported.

Across meta-analyses, process quality and caregiver-child interaction quality have small but fairly consistent positive associations with development.

### C005 — socioeconomic heterogeneity

Supported.

French and Norwegian evidence suggests children from more disadvantaged / lower-parental-education families may receive larger language or achievement benefits from ECEC.

### C006 — more hours cause behavior problems

Not established as a simple dose-response rule.

Older NICHD evidence is model-sensitive and moderated by quality and peer-group context. No universal threshold can be inferred.

### C007 — attachment harm

Not supported as a general main effect.

Large NICHD studies did not find simple main effects of amount or age of entry on mother-child attachment. Quality and maternal sensitivity may interact.

### C008 — cross-country universality

Not supported.

France, Norway, Quebec, Japan and the US differ substantially in childcare systems, quality, baseline home care and policy expansion. Findings should not be transported mechanically.
