## Rechecked claims

### 1. Japan / JECS

Kanamori et al. 2024 full text was rechecked.

Verified:
- N=39,894
- exposure N=13,674
- control N=26,220
- children already using group childcare at 6 months were excluded
- exposed children began after 6 months and by 1 year, then continued through age 3
- inconsistent childcare use between ages 1-3 was excluded
- no ASQ-3 domain difference at 6 months before exposure grouping developed
- at age 3, below-cutoff prevalence:
  - communication 2.1% vs 5.7%
  - gross motor 3.8% vs 5.0%
  - fine motor 7.0% vs 8.4%
  - problem solving 5.6% vs 8.9%
  - personal-social 1.7% vs 5.1%
- multivariable logistic regression retained significant associations in all five domains

Major confounding / selection indicators verified:
- maternal full-time work 59.0% vs 7.0%
- family income <6 million yen 66.4% vs 79.0%
- screen exposure differed substantially
- family time differed substantially
- 60,409 records excluded from the 104,062-record dataset after eligibility/exposure/completeness criteria

Interpretation:
- use association language
- do not adopt the paper's causal-sounding "positive effect" wording as a project conclusion

### 2. ECEC quality — 2023 meta-analysis

von Suchodoletz et al. rechecked.

Verified:
- 8,932 records
- 4,880 unique records
- 185 articles
- 1,044 effect sizes
- ages 0-6
- quality associations:
  - literacy 0.08 (95% CI 0.02-0.13)
  - math 0.07 (0.03-0.10)
  - behavioral skills 0.12 (0.07-0.17)
  - social competence 0.13 (0.07-0.19)
  - behavior problems -0.12 (-0.19 to -0.05)
  - social-emotional problems -0.09 (-0.15 to -0.03)
- process quality showed more consistent associations than structural characteristics
- effects generally small
- evidence base heavily US-weighted

### 3. Longitudinal process quality — 2025

Sluiter et al. rechecked.

Verified:
- 31 publications / 16 longitudinal cohorts
- N=17,913
- aggregate r=0.103
- 95% CI 0.052-0.155
- I2=94.4%

Interpretation:
- small positive longitudinal association
- very high heterogeneity must remain visible

Quality-interpretation correction:
- von Suchodoletz 2023 found significant process-quality associations across many outcomes and no significant pooled associations for structural indicators alone
- however, direct comparisons of process vs structural effect sizes were not significantly different for most outcomes
- final wording therefore avoids claiming that process quality is proven categorically more important than structural quality

### 4. France / Elfe

PubMed abstract rechecked.

Verified:
- instrumental-variable strategy using birth quarter and local crèche supply
- age-1 crèche attendance
- age-2 outcomes
- positive language effect
- no motor effect
- negative behavior effect
- language benefit concentrated among disadvantaged children

Interpretation:
- direct evidence that one exposure can have different outcome directions

### 5. Norway

2023 toddler ECEC expansion study rechecked.

Verified:
- expansion for 1- and 2-year-olds
- linked cohort / administrative achievement outcomes at age 10
- fixed-effects and instrumental-variable approaches
- small / mostly nonsignificant overall effects
- stronger effects for low-parental-education families
- inequality reduction result reported

Havnes & Mogstad 2011 rechecked:
- difference-in-differences
- positive long-run education / labor outcomes
- larger benefit for low-educated-mother households and girls

### 6. Quebec

Baker et al. 2008 / 2019 rechecked:
- policy-expansion studies reported adverse behavioral / health and later noncognitive outcomes

2021 Quebec birth cohort rechecked:
- N=1,450
- no substantial overall behavioral impact at age 6
- slightly higher teacher-rated externalizing outcomes for CPE attendance
- pattern not reproduced in parent reports

Interpretation:
- Quebec evidence is mixed across designs and cohorts
- do not generalize one policy expansion to all childcare

### 7. Hours and behavior

NICHD 2010 rechecked.

Verified:
- causal proposition tests yielded equivocal evidence linking hours to externalizing behavior
- results varied across specifications
- quality and proportion of time in large peer groups moderated associations

Interpretation:
- no universal hours threshold claim

### 8. Attachment

NICHD infant study, N=1,153, rechecked.

Verified:
- no significant main effects of childcare quality, amount, age of entry, stability or type on attachment security / avoidance
- maternal sensitivity / responsiveness mattered
- interaction risks appeared when low maternal sensitivity combined with poor-quality care / other care risks

Age-18 NICHD follow-up, N=857, rechecked.

Verified:
- childcare quantity and type not associated with attachment representations
- quality showed a small positive association with AAI coherence after adjustment
- association was not robust across both attachment measures

Interpretation:
- broad "early daycare damages attachment" claim is not supported
- quality and family caregiving context still matter

### 9. 2024 update evidence

Barry et al. 2024 was independently checked.

Verified:
- six prospective European birth cohorts
- N=87,208
- childcare exposure between ages 0-4
- center-based childcare associated with slightly lower internalizing symptoms across later age groups versus exclusive parental care
- informal childcare showed different patterns, including higher internalizing/externalizing associations at some ages
- 2025 corrigendum exists for the article

French ELFE developmental-delay study 2024 was also checked.

Verified:
- inverse-probability weighted observational comparison
- center-based childcare associated with lower possible developmental-delay risk at age 3.5: OR 0.56 (95% CI 0.51-0.61)
- childminder OR 0.77 (0.72-0.83)
- residual confounding and French-system specificity remain

Interpretation:
- strengthens evidence against a universal harm claim
- does not establish universal causal benefit
- reinforces childcare-type and system-context decomposition
