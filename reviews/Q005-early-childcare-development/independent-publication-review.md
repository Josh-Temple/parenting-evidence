## Claim reconstruction

### C001 — early childcare generally harms development

PASS as not supported as a universal claim.

Japan JECS shows favorable developmental associations at age 3. France and Norway contain favorable language/achievement findings, while Quebec policy-expansion studies report adverse behavioral/noncognitive outcomes. The direction varies across domain and system.

A 2024 IPD meta-analysis of six European birth cohorts (N=87,208) found center-based childcare was associated with slightly lower internalizing symptoms compared with exclusive parental care, while informal childcare showed different/opposite associations for some outcomes.

The correct conclusion is not “no harm anywhere,” but that a universal harm claim is inconsistent with the cross-system evidence.

Sources:
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lanepe.2024.101036
https://doi.org/10.1086/591908
https://doi.org/10.1257/pol.20170603

### C002 — Japanese early-childcare users have more favorable age-3 developmental screening outcomes

PASS as observational association only.

Kanamori et al. (2024), JECS N=39,894:
- early childcare N=13,674
- no childcare N=26,220
- age-3 below-ASQ-cutoff proportions were lower in early-care children in all five domains.

The exposed and control groups differed strongly:
- maternal full-time work 59.0% vs 7.0%
- family income <6 million yen 66.4% vs 79.0%
- screen exposure and family time also differed materially.

Multivariable adjustment reduces measured confounding but does not eliminate unmeasured confounding or selection. The project therefore rejects the paper title’s causal-sounding “positive impact” as its own causal conclusion.

Source:
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-81343-9

### C003 — early childcare generally improves development causally

PASS as not established universally.

French Elfe IV evidence found a positive language effect, no motor effect and a negative behavior effect for crèche attendance at age 1. Norway toddler ECEC expansion showed small/mostly nonsignificant average age-10 achievement effects with larger benefits for low-parental-education families. Quebec policy evidence includes adverse behavioral/noncognitive findings.

A separate 2024 French ELFE observational study found lower developmental-delay risk among center-based childcare users (OR 0.56, 95% CI 0.51–0.61), but residual confounding remains.

These results support outcome- and system-specific conclusions, not a universal causal benefit.

Sources:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33834219/
https://doi.org/10.1080/19345747.2023.2187325
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39214925/

### C004 — higher ECEC quality is associated with better outcomes

PASS_WITH_INTERPRETATION_CHANGE.

von Suchodoletz et al. (2023) included 185 articles and 1,044 effect sizes. Higher ECEC quality was associated with small favorable differences across literacy, math, behavioral skills, social competence, behavioral problems and social-emotional problems.

Process-quality indicators showed significant associations across many outcomes, while structural indicators alone were often not significant. However, direct comparisons between process and structural indicators did not show significantly different effect sizes for most outcomes.

Therefore the Review should say that process quality has fairly consistent small associations, but should not say it is proven categorically more important than structural quality.

Sluiter et al. (2025), 31 publications / 16 longitudinal cohorts / N=17,913, found process quality predicted later socio-emotional development with ES/r=0.103 (95% CI 0.052–0.155), with very high heterogeneity I²=94.4%.

Sources:
https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0285985
https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph22050775

### C005 — effects can differ by family socioeconomic context

PASS.

France Elfe IV evidence found larger language benefits among disadvantaged children. Norway toddler ECEC expansion found stronger achievement benefits for low-parental-education families and evidence of reduced achievement inequality.

These are system-specific heterogeneous treatment effects; they do not prove that every disadvantaged child benefits more in every country.

### C006 — more hours necessarily cause behavior problems

PASS as not established as a simple dose-response rule.

NICHD repeated-measures analyses found evidence relating child-care hours to externalizing behavior was equivocal across model specifications. Associations were moderated by care quality and proportion of time in large peer groups, and magnitudes were modest.

Source:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20053002/

### C007 — early childcare generally damages mother-child attachment

PASS as not supported as a general main effect.

NICHD infant study, N=1,153, found no significant main effects of childcare quality, amount, age of entry, stability or type on attachment security/avoidance. Maternal sensitivity and combined-risk interactions mattered.

NICHD age-18 follow-up, N=857, found quantity and type of early childcare were not associated with attachment representations. Higher observed quality had a small positive association with one measure but not the other.

Sources:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29106728/
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34594056/

### C008 — findings transport unchanged across countries and systems

PASS as not supported.

Japan, France, Norway, Quebec and the US differ in:
- childcare supply and regulation
- baseline alternative care
- quality and staffing
- family selection into care
- age/hours of exposure
- outcomes and informants.

The 2024 six-cohort European IPD analysis also found different patterns for center-based and informal childcare. Country/system and care type must remain explicit modifiers.

## Required changes

1. Refine the 2023 quality-meta-analysis interpretation so it does not imply statistically proven superiority of process over structural quality.
2. Add Barry et al. 2024 six-cohort European IPD meta-analysis as update evidence.
3. Add the 2024 French ELFE developmental-delay study as supportive observational update evidence.
4. Preserve JECS as an observational association, despite causal-sounding wording in the source title.
5. Keep outcome domain, childcare type, socioeconomic context and national system separate.
6. Transition Q005 to PUBLISHED after applying changes.
