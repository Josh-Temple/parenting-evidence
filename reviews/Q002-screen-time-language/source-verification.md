## Rechecked claims

1. Madigan et al. 2020
   - 42-study systematic review/meta-analysis
   - greater quantity of screen use associated with poorer language
   - educational programming and caregiver co-viewing associated with better language
   - verified against JAMA Pediatrics

2. Mallawaarachchi et al. 2024
   - 100 studies, N=176,742; 64 observational studies meta-analyzed
   - program viewing and background TV negatively associated with cognitive outcomes
   - co-use r=0.14 (95% CI 0.03-0.25) for cognitive outcomes
   - verified against JAMA Pediatrics / PubMed

3. Yamamoto et al. 2023 / JECS
   - N=57,980
   - screen age 1 -> communication age 2 beta=-0.06 (95% CI -0.07 to -0.05)
   - communication age 1 -> screen age 2 gamma=-0.03 (-0.04 to -0.02)
   - screen age 2 -> communication age 3 beta=-0.02 (-0.04 to -0.01)
   - communication age 2 -> screen age 3 gamma=-0.06 (-0.07 to -0.04)
   - verified against JAMA Pediatrics
   - supports bidirectionality, not causal certainty

4. Brushe et al. 2024
   - N=220
   - at 36 months each additional minute of screen exposure associated with 6.6 fewer adult words, 4.9 fewer child vocalizations, 1.1 fewer conversational turns
   - verified against JAMA Pediatrics
   - remains observational

5. AAP 2026
   - current policy is Digital Ecosystems, Children, and Adolescents
   - explicitly moves beyond screen limits alone
   - high-quality educational content and joint media engagement may support learning/language
   - verified against current AAP policy and technical report

6. WHO 2019
   - 1-year-olds: sedentary screen time not recommended
   - age 2 and ages 3-4: no more than 1 hour; less is better
   - recommendation integrates movement, sedentary behavior and sleep; it is not a language-specific causal threshold
   - verified against WHO guidance

## Interpretation check

PASS:
- effect sizes are described as associations
- reverse direction is explicitly discussed
- WHO time limit is not presented as a language threshold
- content/context/co-use are separated from total time
- current AAP guidance is used rather than the superseded 2016 statement

## Remaining limitation

The evidence base is predominantly observational and heterogeneous. The page should continue to avoid a universal causal safe-time threshold.
