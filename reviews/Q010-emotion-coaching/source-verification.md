## 1. England-Mason et al. 2023

Sources:
- PMID 36706555
- DOI 10.1016/j.cpr.2023.102252

Verified:
- six databases searched through 2022-10-05
- parents of children age 18 months–6 years 11 months
- 26 reports from 15 randomized trials
- emotion socialization parenting practices: g=0.50
- child emotional competence: g=0.44
- positive parenting behavior: g=0.74
- negative parenting behavior: g=0.25
- parent psychological well-being: g=0.28
- child behavioral adjustment: g=0.34
- main findings remained significant after reported publication-bias/sensitivity analyses
- authors reported no competing interests

Interpretation:
PASS.
This is the primary quantitative anchor for intervention-package efficacy. The effect sizes do not identify the causal contribution of emotion labeling, validation, empathy, or any other single component.

## 2. Johnson et al. 2017

Sources:
- PMID 28414957
- DOI 10.1016/j.cpr.2017.04.001

Verified:
- concurrent association synthesis: 49 studies, n=6,270, r=-0.08
- prospective synthesis: 14 studies, n=1,899, r=-0.11

Interpretation:
PASS as association/prospective background only.
Do not convert the small associations into proof that emotion coaching itself prevents conduct problems.

## 3. Direct TIK / TOTS evidence

Havighurst et al. 2010:
- PMID 20735794
- N=216
- children age 4.0–5.11 years
- 6 sessions + 2 booster sessions
- multicomponent TIK package
- improvements in parent emotion coaching/dismissing, observed emotion discussion/labels, child emotional knowledge and reported behavior outcomes

Wilson et al. effectiveness trial:
- PMID 22182335
- N=128
- age 4.0–5.11 years
- community-practitioner delivery
- randomized intervention vs waitlist
- parent-reported child behavior improved more in intervention
- teacher social competence improved over time in both groups, so not every child outcome was intervention-specific

Tuning in to Toddlers:
- PMID 35007962
- N=300
- age 18–36 months
- cluster randomized
- 6 × 2-hour sessions
- 12-month follow-up
- improvements reported in parent emotion regulation/dismissing/empathy/coaching and child behavior/competence
- parent-report outcomes are prominent

Interpretation:
PASS_WITH_PACKAGE_BOUNDARY.
These support program packages, not one emotion-coaching phrase or isolated component.

## 4. Cross-cultural / update evidence

Hong Kong TIK 2021:
- PMID 34914446
- N=104 parents
- preschoolers age 3–6
- randomized TIK vs waitlist
- direct non-Western intervention evidence

Switzerland online TIK 2024:
- PMID 38424200
- N=141
- age 3–6
- randomized online TIK vs waitlist
- reported improvements in emotion-related parent/family measures and small child-behavior effects

China pilot 2025:
- PMID 39871019
- N=73 parents of preschoolers
- 4-week emotion-focused parenting program + shared reading vs waitlist
- some immediate reductions in punitive/minimizing reactions and dysfunctional interactions occurred in both intervention and waitlist groups
- selected delayed effects were reported on some parent beliefs/reactions and parenting stress

Interpretation:
PASS.
The China pilot remains mixed and is not summarized as uniformly positive.

## 5. Comparator strength / evidence independence

Broader emotionally oriented parenting review (Zahl-Olsen et al. 2023):
- PMID 37519350
- 33 included studies
- 25/33 examined Tuning in to Kids variants
- Sophie Havighurst was an author on 23/25 TIK studies according to the review
- 23 studies used randomized designs
- among those randomized studies: 14 waitlist controls and 9 other controls

Interpretation:
PASS_WITH_LIMITATION.
Developer/research-team concentration does not invalidate results, but paper/study count must not be treated as the number of fully independent intervention traditions.

The evidence base supports benefit relative to waitlist/no comparable intervention in many studies. Consistent superiority over another credible active parenting program is not established.

## 6. Japan applicability

Japan observational study:
- PMID 33013514
- 300 Japanese-speaking married mothers
- children age 2–5
- maternal emotion regulation, anxiety/depression and parenting alliance were associated with emotion coaching/dismissing patterns

Interpretation:
Japan-specific construct/context evidence only; not intervention efficacy.
