# Q009 Independent Publication Review

Date: 2026-09-26  
Mode: independent reconstruction from major sources; existing Review conclusions were not assumed correct  
Verdict: PASS_WITH_CHANGES  
Final state after required changes: REVIEW

## Scope

The central claims were reconstructed from:
- Mindell et al. 2009 randomized routine study
- Mindell et al. 2015 multinational observational study
- Fiese et al. 2021 longitudinal cohort
- Yamamoto & Hara 2024 Japan preschool study
- Mindell et al. 2026 primary-care pilot RCT
- Pétrin et al. 2026 systematic review of early-childhood behavioral sleep interventions

The existing Reader Layer was assessed after the main evidence boundaries were reconstructed.

## Independent bottom line

A balanced parent-facing conclusion is:

> 乳児・幼児では、consistent bedtime routineに短期的な睡眠改善を支持する直接介入研究がある。一方、その直接RCTは短期間・親報告中心で、baseline imbalanceと分析上の制約もあり、近年のpilot RCTではoverall main effectが確認されていない。大規模観察・longitudinal evidenceはroutine consistencyとより良いsleepの関連を支持するが、因果的な頻度thresholdは示さない。日本の4〜6歳ではoverall routine indexとsleep disturbanceの明確な関連は確認されていない。したがって、短期の乳幼児sleep benefit、個別component、日本の就学前児への一般化、broader developmentを分けて扱う必要がある。

This supports a bounded sleep claim, not a universal routine prescription.

## Claim reconstruction

### C001 — short-term infant/toddler sleep

Decision: PASS_WITH_CHANGES

Mindell 2009 provides direct randomized intervention evidence:
- N=405
- 7–36 months
- 3-week design
- intervention groups improved in SOL, night waking and continuity
- maternal mood also improved

However:
- several baseline sleep variables differed despite randomization
- analyses/reporting emphasized within-group repeated change
- outcomes were parent-reported
- routine was a specific multicomponent package
- study duration was short
- commercial funding / author ties were substantial

Required interpretation:
Use “direct short-term intervention evidence supports benefit” rather than implying a robust, transportable between-group effect estimate.

The current Reader Layer reflects this.

### C002 — consistency/frequency

Decision: PASS

Mindell 2015 and Fiese 2021 support association / temporal pattern:
- large multinational association
- longitudinal partial cross-lag relationships

They do not establish:
- a universal minimum number of nights
- a biological frequency threshold
- one-way causality

The Reader Layer keeps this as association evidence.

### C003 — one optimal routine package

Decision: PASS

No reviewed evidence identifies one mandatory bath/read/cuddle sequence.

Pétrin 2026 confirms that bedtime routine is commonly embedded in broader behavioral sleep interventions, but the review does not isolate the effect of bedtime routine alone.

The Reader Layer correctly uses Pétrin as methodological/background context only.

### C004 — screen/device avoidance

Decision: PASS_WITH_CONTEXT

Yamamoto & Hara 2024:
- device avoidance β=-0.222, p=0.011 for sleep disturbance
- item-level result is exploratory and cross-sectional

This can support component-specific plausibility but not the claim that device avoidance explains the whole routine effect.

### C005 — broader development / behavior

Decision: PASS

The evidence base contains conceptual and observational links, but general causal developmental benefit from bedtime-routine intervention is not established.

Mindell 2026 had no overall social-emotional main effect.

The current Reader Layer does not upgrade broader developmental associations into intervention causality.

### C006 — caregiver mood/stress

Decision: PASS_WITH_CONTEXT

Mindell 2009 supports short-term maternal mood improvement.

Mindell 2026 had no overall caregiver-stress main effect; lower-education interaction signals were exploratory.

A general or long-term caregiver mental-health benefit is not established.

### C007 — Japan applicability

Decision: PASS

Yamamoto & Hara 2024 directly limits generalization:
- overall BTR index → sleep disturbance β=-0.013, p=0.736
- overall BTR index → SDQ total difficulties β=-0.063, p=0.094

The Reader Layer retains this overall-null Japan result prominently.

### C008 — bedtime routine vs sleep training

Decision: PASS

The review keeps pre-sleep routine separate from extinction / graduated extinction / bedtime fading and other Q004 intervention categories.

## Required challenge

Question:
Would the Reader Layer remain balanced if Mindell 2009 were removed?

Decision:
**YES, but the causal wording would weaken materially.**

Without Mindell 2009:
- Mindell 2015 and Fiese 2021 still support association / longitudinal pattern
- Yamamoto & Hara 2024 remains an important Japan overall-null boundary
- Mindell 2026 remains a randomized overall-null update
- Pétrin 2026 shows bedtime routines are common in broader intervention packages but does not isolate efficacy

Therefore the conclusion is not wholly dependent on the 2009 study, but the direct-efficacy wording is.

This prevents one short sponsored RCT from silently carrying the entire review.

## Funding / evidence-independence assessment

The evidence network is not fully independent.

Material context:
- 2009 study: Johnson & Johnson support and multiple company-affiliated authors
- 2015 study: Johnson & Johnson Asia Pacific affiliation / sponsorship context
- 2026 pilot: partial Simms/Mann and Johnson & Johnson Consumer funding; Kenvue consulting disclosure

Decision:
Retain these studies, but do not count repeated results from the same network as equivalent to multiple independent replications.

The current Evidence Table and Reader Layer preserve this boundary.

## Required changes

1. Record the 2009 RCT baseline imbalance and within-group analysis limitation.
2. Keep the 2024 Japan overall-null result prominent.
3. Keep the 2026 pilot overall-null result primary; subgroup signals remain exploratory.
4. Use Pétrin 2026 only as methodological/background evidence.
5. Avoid treating repeated studies from an overlapping research/funding network as fully independent replication.

All five changes are reflected in the current branch.
