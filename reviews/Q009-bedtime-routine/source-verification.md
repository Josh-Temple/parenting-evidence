## 1. Mindell et al. 2009

Sources:
- PMID 19480226
- PMCID PMC2675894
- DOI 10.1093/sleep/32.5.599

Verified:
- N=405 with complete data
- infants 7–18m: n=206
- toddlers 18–36m: n=199
- randomized routine vs control/usual-routine assignment
- 3-week design
- parent-report BISQ / sleep diary outcomes
- improvements within intervention groups in SOL, waking number/duration and continuity
- maternal mood improved
- control groups did not show comparable longitudinal change
- baseline sleep differences existed between randomized groups for several variables
- analyses/reporting emphasized repeated measures within each group
- routine was a specific multicomponent package
- three authors were Johnson & Johnson Consumer Companies employees; commercial support/context is material

Interpretation:
PASS_WITH_BOUNDARY.
Use as direct short-term intervention evidence, but not as a single clean between-group causal effect estimate and not as component-specific proof.

## 2. Mindell et al. 2015

Sources:
- PMID 25325483
- PMCID PMC4402657
- DOI 10.5665/sleep.4662

Verified:
- N=10,085
- age 0–5
- multinational sample including Japan
- routine consistency/frequency associated with earlier bedtime, shorter SOL, fewer night wakings and longer sleep
- dose-related association reported
- observational design
- one author affiliated with Johnson & Johnson Asia Pacific

Interpretation:
PASS as association evidence.
Do not turn frequency categories into a biological threshold.

## 3. Fiese et al. 2021 longitudinal cohort

Sources:
- PMID 33624804
- DOI 10.1093/sleep/zsab045

Verified:
- n=468
- 3 / 12 / 18 / 24 month repeated assessments
- partial reciprocal cross-lag associations
- more routine consistency predicted less night waking / fewer sleep problems in some models
- adaptive activities predicted longer sleep / fewer sleep problems in some models

Interpretation:
PASS as longitudinal observational evidence; reciprocal pathways remain plausible.

## 4. Yamamoto & Hara 2024 — Japan

Sources:
- PMID 39300986
- PMCID PMC11408423
- DOI 10.1007/s41105-024-00530-3

Verified:
- N=700 caregivers of children age 4–6
- cross-sectional web survey
- overall BTR index → sleep disturbance: β=-0.013, p=0.736
- overall BTR index → SDQ total difficulties: β=-0.063, p=0.094
- exploratory device avoidance → sleep disturbance: β=-0.222, p=0.011
- exploratory reading/story → sleep disturbance: β=+0.228, p=0.006

Interpretation:
PASS.
Overall Japan preschool result is null.
Reading/story coefficient is not interpreted causally as harm.
Device-avoidance result remains component-specific exploratory association.

## 5. Mindell et al. 2026 pilot RCT

Sources:
- PMID 41567595
- PMCID PMC12815716
- DOI 10.3389/frsle.2025.1722530

Verified:
- 100 randomized
- 86 included in analyses
- mean toddler age 12.89m
- 62 completed both follow-ups
- no overall group differences in sleep outcomes at 15 or 24m
- no overall main effects for social-emotional outcomes or caregiver stress
- usual-care missingness 52% vs intervention 28%, p=0.014
- educational-attainment interaction analyses were exploratory and used p<0.10 due limited power
- reading increased in intervention families at 15m
- bath/toothbrushing differences were not significant at 15m
- component differences did not remain at 24m
- partial funding from Simms/Mann Family Foundation and Johnson & Johnson Consumer Inc.
- funders reported no involvement in design/data/analysis/interpretation/writing/submission
- Jodi Mindell disclosed other unrestricted grants and consulting for Kenvue Brands LLC

Interpretation:
PASS_WITH_BOUNDARY.
Overall null remains primary; exploratory subgroup signals do not override it.

## 6. Pétrin et al. 2026 systematic review

Sources:
- PMID 41996955
- DOI 10.1016/j.sleep.2026.108966

Verified:
- 59 early-childhood behavioral sleep intervention studies
- bedtime routines addressed in 53%
- sleep independence/self-soothing 68%
- sleep hygiene 47%
- review maps intervention content/delivery/outcomes

Interpretation:
PASS as methodological/background evidence only.
It does not isolate bedtime-routine efficacy.
