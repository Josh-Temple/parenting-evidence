### 2. Appleton et al. 2018 — pooled effect sizes

Sources:
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30321277/
- https://doi.org/10.1093/ajcn/nqy143
- erratum: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30517611/

Verified:
- 43 articles / 117 comparisons
- repeated exposure vs no exposure:
  - liking SMD 0.35; 95% CI 0.17–0.53; 17 comparisons / 820 participants
  - intake SMD 0.23; 95% CI 0.07–0.39; 21 comparisons / 1,130 participants
- authors characterize effects as small and long-term evidence as limited
- erratum corrects in-text citations and wording; does not report corrections to these extracted effect estimates

Interpretation:
- supports average target-vegetable effect
- does not show every child benefits or that picky eating overall resolves
- Reader Layer wording within scope: YES

### 3. Shapiro et al. 2026 — schedule evidence

Source:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41903739/

Verified:
- RCT
- 59 infant-caregiver dyads enrolled; 54 completed
- mean infant age 11 months
- artichoke; all schedules targeted 10 exposures
- intensive 2-week schedule: 26.8 g to 22.9 g
- take-a-break: 21.1 g to 43.6 g
- extended: 20.7 g to 33.6 g

Interpretation:
- supports C003 that total count alone is insufficient
- single small RCT does not establish universal spacing schedule
- Reader Layer wording within scope: YES

### 4. Fleischeuer et al. 2026 — reinforcement add-on

Source:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41577139/

Verified:
- preregistered within-subject study
- N=62; age 4–6
- 10-day intervention
- positive non-food reinforcement, negative reinforcement, exposure, control
- positive reinforcement produced highest post-intervention intake; liking benefit strongest in positive-reinforcement condition

Interpretation:
- supports strategy-specific possible add-on benefit
- not a universal recommendation to use rewards
- food bribes are outside this comparison
- Reader Layer wording within scope: YES

### 5. Karagiannaki et al. 2021 — exposure frequency

Source:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33919386/

Verified:
- N=159; age 3–6
- seven exposures delivered at different frequencies
- liking/intake increased
- no particular exposure frequency could be recommended
- intake plateau around exposure 4 was study-specific

Interpretation:
- no universal 4-times threshold
- Reader Layer wording within scope: YES

### 6. Ahern 2019 / Zeinstra 2018 — persistence and contradictory evidence

Sources:
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30290205/
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29158150/

Verified:
- Ahern: N=95; age 24–55m; 5–6 exposures; target intake increased; n=40 follow-up remained above baseline at 1 month
- Zeinstra: N=250; repeated exposure improved pumpkin and white radish intake but not courgette

Interpretation:
- persistence evidence is limited
- food-specific null result retained
- Reader Layer wording within scope: YES

### 7. Clinical boundary — Goday et al. 2019 / Estrem et al. 2025 / 2026 treatment consensus

Sources:
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30358739/
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39679744/
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42178663/

Verified:
- Goday 2019: PFD is defined as impaired oral intake that is not age-appropriate and associated with medical, nutritional, feeding-skill and/or psychosocial dysfunction.
- Estrem 2025: PFD and ARFID have areas of diagnostic overlap and distinction.
- 2026 treatment consensus: structured exposure to food/feeding experiences can be a common psychological treatment element across PFD/ARFID, with delivery depending on severity, age/development, and target behavior.

Interpretation:
- ordinary picky eating and clinical feeding/eating disorders remain separate review layers
- generic home repeated offering is not equivalent to structured clinical exposure treatment
- Reader Layer wording within scope: YES
