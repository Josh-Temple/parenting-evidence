## Interpretation by claim

### C001 — parenting programs work

Supported.

Behavioral parenting programs reduce disruptive/conduct problems in the short term. This is stronger evidence than the evidence for any one discipline technique.

### C002 — time-out has an independent component effect

Plausible, but attribution is limited.

An older component meta-analysis associated teaching time-out with larger program effects. A newer and larger component meta-analysis did not identify time-out among the individual techniques consistently associated with stronger outcomes. Neither design randomizes time-out while holding all other program elements constant.

### C003 — direct target-behavior reduction

Supported by direct but older/small experimental literature.

Time-out can reduce noncompliance and other carefully defined target behaviors. The strongest limitation is that many direct studies are small, old or use single-case/applied-behavior designs.

### C004 — attachment / long-term harm

A blanket harm claim is not supported by available evidence.

Longitudinal observational data have not shown worse long-term emotional, behavioral or parent-child relationship outcomes among time-out users. More recent cross-sectional data suggest implementation quality matters. These findings are reassuring but do not establish that every form of isolation labeled "time-out" is safe.

### C005 — implementation matters

Supported.

Evidence-based time-out is brief, predictable, calm, linked to a known target behavior, safe, and embedded in a broader pattern of positive attention and reinforcement. Extended isolation, anger, humiliation, fear, deprivation, unpredictable use or physical force should not be treated as equivalent to evidence-based time-out.

### C006 — trauma/adversity contraindication

Not supported as a universal rule.

A clinical study of children with high adversity exposure did not show worse outcomes in a multicomponent program that included time-out. Because the technique was not isolated, individual assessment remains appropriate when trauma-related distress is prominent.

### C007 — emotional distress

Not supported as an automatic indication.

Current CDC guidance distinguishes deliberate dangerous/harmful/rule-breaking/noncompliant behavior from emotional distress. When a child is overwhelmed or distressed, supportive regulation or "time-in" may be more appropriate.
