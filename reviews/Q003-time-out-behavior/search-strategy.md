# Q003 Search Strategy

Status: EXECUTED WITH LIMITATION

Search date: 2026-09-22

## Review question

幼児〜就学前児の問題行動に対し、time-out from positive reinforcementは有効か。また、適切に実施した場合にattachmentや長期的な情緒・行動上の害は確認されているか。

## Core concepts

```text
("time-out" OR "time out" OR "timeout" OR "time-out from positive reinforcement")
AND
(child* OR preschool* OR toddler*)
AND
(disruptive behavior OR conduct problem* OR noncompliance OR aggression
 OR parent training OR parent management training)
```

Safety search:

```text
("time-out" OR "time out")
AND
(child* OR preschool*)
AND
(attachment OR mental health OR emotional functioning OR adversity
 OR trauma OR long-term OR harm)
```

## Key methodological distinction

Q003では、次の3種類の証拠を分ける。

1. **Program-level evidence**
   - time-outを含むことがあるbehavioral parent training全体の効果
2. **Component-level evidence**
   - time-outを含むプログラムの方が効果が大きいか等を比較するmeta-regression / component analysis
3. **Technique-level direct evidence**
   - time-out自体を実際に操作・比較した研究

Program-levelの効果をtime-out単独の効果として扱わない。

## Main evidence anchors

### 1. Parenting-program effectiveness

Cochrane 2022:
- behavioral / cognitive-behavioral group parenting programs
- children age 3-12 with conduct problems
- 13 trials (10 RCTs + 3 quasi-randomized)
- N=1,078
- parent-rated conduct problems: SMD -0.53 (95% CI -0.72 to -0.34)
- independent assessment: SMD -0.44 (-0.77 to -0.11)
- short-term benefit supported
- long-term evidence unavailable

Source:
https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD008225_group-parenting-programmes-improving-behavioural-problems-children-aged-3-12-years

### 2. Component evidence

Kaminski et al. 2008:
- meta-analysis of 77 parent-training evaluations for children age 0-7
- after controlling for research-design differences, larger effects were associated with:
  - positive parent-child interaction / emotional communication
  - teaching time-out
  - parenting consistency
  - in-session practice
- observational component meta-regression: does not randomize time-out itself

Source:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18205039/

Leijten et al. 2019:
- 154 randomized trials / 398 effect sizes for program effectiveness
- 26 parenting techniques coded
- stronger effects most consistently associated with:
  - positive reinforcement
  - praise
  - natural / logical consequences
- time-out was not identified among the three components associated with stronger effects
- no individual technique predicted stronger long-term effects

Source:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30738545/

Leijten et al. 2022:
- network meta-analysis of 197 randomized trials
- focused behavior-management programs were effective and among the most promising program types
- component clusters, not time-out in isolation, were compared

Source:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34240409/

### 3. Direct time-out evidence

Leijten et al. 2018:
- systematic review + multilevel meta-analysis of experimentally manipulated discrete parenting behaviors
- 19 studies / 75 effect sizes across praise, verbal reprimands, time-out and ignore
- included studies required experimental manipulation of a single parenting behavior; mean child age 2-9 years
- time-out for noncompliance increased observed and parent-reported child compliance
- reported effect sizes for time-out: d=0.84-1.72; 95% CIs across estimates approximately 0.30-2.54
- major limitation: relatively few direct experiments, many conducted decades ago; outcome is short-term compliance rather than broad long-term functioning

Source:
https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0204929

Gardner et al. 1976:
- N=32 mother-child pairs
- compared control, time-out, explanation before, explanation after
- time-out increased child compliance
- adding an explanation did not further improve effectiveness
- very small, old experimental study; external validity limited

Source:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/972210/

Later applied behavior-analysis studies also support immediate reductions in specific target behaviors, but samples are generally very small and do not establish population-level long-term effect sizes.

### 4. Long-term / attachment safety evidence

Knight et al. 2020:
- archival longitudinal Early Head Start data
- parent-reported time-out use at 36 months
- child emotional / behavioral health and parent-child relationship measured through pre-K and fifth grade
- no significant differences in measured outcomes between children whose parents reported time-out and those who did not
- observational exposure; implementation quality not randomized

Source:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31513092/

Roach et al. 2025:
- nationally representative Australian cross-sectional sample
- N=474 caregivers of children age 6-8
- appropriately implemented time-out associated with better attachment / mental-health measures
- inappropriate implementation associated with worse outcomes
- cross-sectional, self-report; cannot establish causality

Source:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39632609/

### 5. Trauma / adversity

Roach et al. 2022:
- nonrandomized waitlist-controlled clinical study
- N=205, age 2-9, conduct problems
- 10-session parent-management program including reinforcement, limit setting and time-out
- children with high adversity exposure experienced similar or greater overall benefit than low-adversity children
- time-out effect cannot be isolated from the full program

Source:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36048440/

### 6. Current implementation guidance

CDC 2026:
- time-out is not for every misbehavior
- candidates include dangerous behavior, harmful behavior, family-rule violations and continued noncompliance after a warning
- keep it brief, safe and distraction-free
- about 1 minute per year of age
- avoid lecturing, scolding and arguing
- resume positive reinforcement afterward
- when a child is in emotional distress, time-out may not be best; consider supportive "time-in"

Source:
https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/responding-to-behavior/time-out.html

AAP:
- continues to present time-out as a nonphysical discipline option
- advises positive discipline and rejects corporal punishment, threatening, humiliation and shaming

Sources:
https://publications.aap.org/pediatrics/article/142/6/e20183112/37452/Effective-Discipline-to-Raise-Healthy-Children
https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/Time-Outs-101.aspx

### 7. Japanese context

厚生労働省のペアレント・トレーニング関連資料では、タイムアウトを、環境調整・明確な指示を行っても危険・他害行動等が続く場合に、問題行動直後に好ましい結果から一時的に離す方法として説明している。怒鳴ったり長く説教したりしないことも記載されている。

Source:
https://www.mhlw.go.jp/content/12200000/000653549.pdf

こども家庭庁は、しつけと体罰を明確に区別し、体罰・暴言ではなく、子どもの気持ち、発達、環境調整、肯定的で具体的な声かけ、できている行動をほめることを重視している。

Source:
https://kodomoshien.cfa.go.jp/no-gyakutai/no-taibatsu/

## Search limitation

今回もde novo systematic search、database export、独自deduplication、二重screeningは行っていない。

また、time-out単独研究には古い小規模研究が多く、modern parenting-program RCTの多くは複数技法をセットで評価している。

したがって、
- parent-training overall effect
- time-out component association
- time-out direct effect
- safety evidence

を明確に分けて解釈する。
