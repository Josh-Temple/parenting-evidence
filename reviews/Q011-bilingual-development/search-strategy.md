# Q011 Search Strategy

Last searched: 2026-09-26

## Review type

Targeted evidence review. This is **not** a de novo systematic review.

Primary question:
Does regular early exposure to two spoken languages itself cause a global early-language delay?

## Search streams

1. early bilingual language milestones
2. language-specific vocabulary and input quantity/quality
3. total / conceptual vocabulary measurement
4. dual-language input / code-switching / OPOL
5. bilingual language-disorder assessment
6. Japan-specific Japanese-English evidence
7. passive media boundary

## Key fresh check — 2026-09-26

Muszyńska et al. 2025:
- longitudinal
- 302 bilingual / 302 matched monolingual
- no clear timing difference for several core early-language milestones
- strongest no-difference evidence for first word / 10th word / early multiword production
- later milestone evidence more limited

Fibla et al. 2022:
- each language develops as a function of quantity and quality of experience
- bilingual experience is highly variable
- code-switching is a normal bilingual phenomenon
- no single family exposure pattern is universally required

Core et al. 2013:
- cross-language vocabulary measures are important for assessment
- one-language vocabulary alone may not reflect total bilingual lexical knowledge

Kaushanskaya 2023:
- dual-language input structure can matter in experimental learning
- evidence does not justify one universal bilingual exposure strategy

## Inclusion / exclusion

Include:
- typically developing early bilingual children
- longitudinal milestone/exposure evidence
- cross-language vocabulary assessment
- relevant DLD assessment boundary

Separate:
- early-English proficiency advantage (Q012)
- cognitive advantage claims
- passive English media efficacy
- school-age immersion outcomes
- language-disorder treatment

## Interpretation rules

- global delay ≠ smaller vocabulary in one language
- input quantity/quality affects each language
- total/conceptual vocabulary ≠ guaranteed equivalence at all ages
- lab switching cost ≠ developmental delay
- bilingualism does not rule out genuine DLD
- Japan-targeted-search absence ≠ proof no study exists
- passive media ≠ interactive bilingual exposure

## Limitations

- no database-native full export
- no dual screening
- strongest direct evidence is mostly non-Japanese language pairs
- exposure definitions vary
- parent-report milestone data have measurement/attrition limitations
