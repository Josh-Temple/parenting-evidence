## 1. Muszyńska et al. 2025 — early milestones

Sources:
- PMID 39810738
- Journal of Child Language, published online 2025-01-15

Verified:
- longitudinal parent-report design using a mobile app
- 302 Polish bilingual children living outside Poland
- 302 matched Polish monolingual children
- mean age at entry 12.78 months; range 0–24 months
- bilingual and monolingual groups did not differ clearly in the age of reaching several early language milestones
- strongest/most robust no-difference evidence included first word, 10th word and first multiword utterance
- babbling also showed no clear group difference
- 50th-word evidence was less definitive / inconclusive
- bilingual milestone scoring considered production across both languages

Interpretation:
PASS.
This is strong evidence against a simple early global-delay claim. It is not proof that Japanese-only vocabulary or every later-language measure is identical to monolingual peers.

## 2. Fibla et al. 2022 — input quantity / quality

Sources:
- PMID 35224184
- PMCID PMC8866745
- DOI 10.1177/23727322211069312

Verified:
- bilingual development varies substantially across children and contexts
- success in each language depends on quantity and quality of everyday language experience
- high-quality interactive exposure in each language is emphasized
- code-switching is discussed as a normal bilingual communicative resource; laboratory switching costs do not justify a developmental-harm claim

Interpretation:
PASS.
The Reader Layer correctly says that bilingualism itself is not a simple global-delay mechanism while each language remains exposure-dependent.

## 3. Core et al. 2013 — cross-language vocabulary measurement

Sources:
- PMID 24023382
- PMCID PMC4337205
- DOI 10.1044/1092-4388(2013/11-0044)

Verified:
- 47 Spanish–English bilingual children
- 22–30 months
- compared total vocabulary and conceptual vocabulary with monolingual single-language assessment logic
- purpose directly concerns bilingual vocabulary assessment and risk of under-representing bilingual knowledge when relying on one language alone

Interpretation:
PASS_WITH_MEASUREMENT_BOUNDARY.
Total vocabulary and conceptual vocabulary are useful cross-language measures, but they do not justify a universal claim that every bilingual child is exactly equivalent to monolingual peers on every measure.

## 4. Kaushanskaya 2023 — dual-language input strategy

Source:
- DOI 10.1044/2023_JSLHR-23-00181

Verified:
- review focuses on experimental evidence about how dual-language input format shapes learning
- bilingual–monolingual comparisons alone are insufficient for prescribing an optimal family exposure strategy
- evidence does not establish one universally optimal OPOL / mixed-language strategy

Interpretation:
PASS.
Laboratory switch costs or novel-word-learning effects are not converted into a claim that naturalistic code-switching causes developmental delay.
