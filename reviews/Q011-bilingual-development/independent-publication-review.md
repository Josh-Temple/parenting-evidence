## Independent bottom line

A defensible conclusion is:

> Meaningful early exposure to two spoken languages does not show a simple global-delay signal for early milestones when both languages are considered. However, skill in each language depends on the amount and quality of experience in that language, so Japanese-only vocabulary can differ from a Japanese-only monolingual peer without implying a global language disorder. Assessment should consider both languages, exposure history, and functional communication. Passive media should not be treated as equivalent to interactive bilingual exposure.

This supports a bounded “no simple global delay” claim, not a claim that Japanese language scores are unaffected by bilingual exposure allocation.

## Claim reconstruction

### C001 — early bilingual exposure causes a global early-language delay

Decision: NOT SUPPORTED / NO SIMPLE DELAY SIGNAL

Muszyńska et al. 2025:
- 302 bilingual + 302 matched monolingual children
- no clear difference in timing for babbling, first word, 10th word and first multiword utterance
- strongest evidence for no difference in first word / 10th word / early multiword production
- 50th-word evidence less definitive
- bilingual production counted across both languages

Boundary:
This does not establish identical Japanese-only proficiency or identical later vocabulary distributions.

### C002 / C004 — language-specific skill and exposure

Decision: PASS

Fibla et al. 2022 supports the central exposure principle:
- quantity and quality of experience matter for each language
- development is highly variable across bilingual children and contexts

Therefore:
- less Japanese input can be associated with less Japanese-specific vocabulary growth
- this is not equivalent to saying English “damages” Japanese or causes global delay

### C003 — combined language knowledge

Decision: PASS_WITH_MEASUREMENT_BOUNDARY

Core et al. 2013 supports use of total and conceptual vocabulary to understand bilingual lexical knowledge.

Required caution:
- total vocabulary and conceptual vocabulary are different measures
- neither guarantees exact equivalence with monolingual children at every age
- one-language scores can underrepresent cross-language knowledge

The Reader Layer preserves this.

### C005 — OPOL is necessary or best

Decision: NOT ESTABLISHED

Kaushanskaya 2023 shows that input format can affect experimental learning, but the evidence does not establish one universally optimal family strategy.

OPOL is therefore not presented as the only evidence-based approach.

### C006 — code-switching causes delay

Decision: NOT ESTABLISHED

Naturalistic code-switching is part of bilingual communication.

Laboratory switching costs in some tasks are not evidence that mixed-language family interaction causes developmental delay.

### C007 — bilingualism explains all apparent delay

Decision: FALSE AS A CLINICAL ASSUMPTION

Bilingual children can have genuine persistent language difficulties.

Assessment should consider:
- both/all languages
- exposure history
- functional communication
- developmental history
- concerns across contexts

The Reader Layer preserves this clinical boundary.

### C008 — Japan applicability

Decision: LIMITED DIRECT EVIDENCE

The strongest direct milestone and vocabulary evidence comes from other language pairs.

Current targeted search did not identify a strong Japanese-primary + early-English causal comparison with Japanese development as the primary outcome.

This remains a targeted-search limitation, not a claim of nonexistence.

### C009 — passive media is equivalent to interactive bilingual exposure

Decision: NOT ESTABLISHED / OUTSIDE PRIMARY SCOPE

The core bilingual-development evidence concerns meaningful language experience.

It does not support treating background audio/video or passive English media as equivalent to caregiver/childcare interaction.

## Required challenge 1

Question:
**Does “no simple global delay” imply that early English has no effect at all on Japanese-specific language scores?**

Decision:
**NO.**

Language-specific development remains exposure-dependent. The Reader Layer must preserve the possibility that Japanese-only vocabulary differs when time/input is distributed across two languages.

## Required challenge 2

Question:
**Would the conclusion remain balanced without the 2025 milestone study?**

Decision:
**YES, but the direct milestone claim would weaken.**

Fibla 2022, language-specific exposure research, and bilingual vocabulary/assessment literature still argue against treating bilingualism as a deficit and support cross-language assessment. The 2025 study provides unusually direct recent evidence on milestone timing and therefore should not carry claims beyond those milestones.

## Required challenge 3

Question:
**Can this evidence be used to recommend English videos/audio as an early bilingual strategy?**

Decision:
**NO.**

Interactive spoken-language exposure and passive media are different exposures. Q011 cannot infer media efficacy from bilingual interaction evidence.

## Required changes

1. Clarify in the opening that the primary evidence concerns meaningful, ongoing bilingual interaction/exposure, not passive background media.
2. Keep Japanese-only vocabulary and global developmental delay separate.
3. Keep 50th-word/later milestone uncertainty visible.
4. Keep total vs conceptual vocabulary as measurement tools, not exact-equivalence claims.
5. Keep Japan direct-evidence limitation and DLD boundary explicit.

All five changes are reflected in the current branch.
