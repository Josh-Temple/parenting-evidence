## Interpretation by claim

### C001 — short-term sleep improvement after about 6 months

Supported, with heterogeneity.

Multiple systematic reviews and RCTs indicate improvement in sleep onset, night waking, or continuous sleep. Effects vary by intervention and measurement method.

### C002 — large increase in total sleep

Not supported as a general average claim.

A broad RCT meta-analysis found only about 9 additional minutes of nocturnal sleep per night. More recent digital-intervention evidence similarly separates longer continuous sleep from unchanged total night-time sleep.

### C003 — physiological stress increase

Not supported by the available direct RCT, but evidence is limited.

The small Gradisar RCT found cortisol declines rather than increases. This single small trial cannot establish a general absence of stress effects across all methods.

### C004 — long-term emotional / attachment harm

A broad harm claim is not supported by available follow-up evidence.

One small RCT showed no 12-month attachment/emotional difference and a separate cluster-RCT follow-up found no measured harm signal at age 6. Samples, attrition and intervention specificity limit certainty.

### C005 — under 6 months

Insufficient evidence for extending the same recommendation.

Reuter 2020 identifies <6 months as a major knowledge gap. The older Douglas review specifically did not find established benefit from population-level behavioral sleep interventions in the first six months.

### C006 — one best method

Not established.

Interventions combine techniques and no clear universal winner has been established.

### C007 — safe sleep rules can be relaxed

Not supported.

Behavioral sleep efficacy does not override SIDS / sleep-environment guidance. Safe sleep remains a separate, higher-priority safety constraint.

### C008 — parent outcomes

Partially supported.

Recent meta-analyses report improvements in maternal mental health / confidence, but effect estimates and intervention types are heterogeneous.
