# Q004 Source Verification — Pass 1

Date: 2026-09-23  
Result: PASS_WITH_LIMITATIONS_FOR_REVIEW  
Publication status: independent publication review completed 2026-09-23; see `independent-publication-review.md`

## Rechecked claims

### 1. Short-term efficacy after about 6 months

Reuter et al. 2020 was rechecked against the open publisher full text.

Verified:
- MEDLINE, Web of Science and PsycINFO searched in December 2017
- 2,070 unique records initially screened
- 476 reports of original studies assessed in full text
- 12 original intervention studies included
- five behavioral interventions using routines / controlled crying showed modest short-term effects
- the authors explicitly describe behavioral strategies as promising for infants above 6 months
- <6 months and consequences of extinction-containing interventions were identified as knowledge gaps

Interpretation:
- supports a cautious short-term efficacy claim
- does not support one universally superior method
- does not justify extending the conclusion to newborns / young infants

### 2. Contemporary systematic-review consistency

Stanzel et al. 2025 was rechecked against the publisher abstract/full-text metadata.

Verified:
- search conducted November 2020 and updated May 2023
- 22 papers reporting 20 studies included
- behavioral interventions were associated with broadly mild-to-moderate improvements in unsettled infant behavior, including night waking / cry-fuss behavior
- maternal mental health / parenting confidence also improved in the synthesis
- substantial clinical / intervention heterogeneity remains

Interpretation:
- direction is consistent with Reuter 2020
- heterogeneity prevents a single-method recommendation

### 3. Total sleep duration is not the same as settling / consolidation

Fangupo et al. 2021 was rechecked against PubMed.

Verified:
- 32 RCTs included
- nocturnal sleep duration increased by mean 9 min/night
- 95% CI 4.1 to 13.8 min
- 24-hour sleep change +8.6 min, 95% CI -2.7 to 19.8
- no evidence of changed daytime sleep duration

Uzdil et al. 2025 was also rechecked.

Verified:
- 4 RCTs of digitally delivered behavioral interventions
- longest continuous sleep period improved
- total night-time sleep did not clearly improve
- all included studies rated high risk of bias
- certainty described as low

Interpretation:
- the public page should not imply large increases in total sleep time
- sleep onset, awakenings, continuous sleep and total duration must remain separate outcomes

### 4. Objective-measure evidence

Chae et al. 2024 was rechecked against PubMed.

Verified:
- 11 studies using actigraphy
- fairly consistent effects on number of awakenings and sleep onset latency
- only 2 studies were judged low risk of bias
- parental psychosocial outcomes were inconsistent

Interpretation:
- supports short-term sleep improvement while preserving risk-of-bias caution

### 5. Cortisol / attachment / 12-month outcomes

Gradisar et al. 2016 was rechecked against PubMed / Pediatrics.

Verified:
- N=43, age 6-16 months
- graduated extinction n=14
- bedtime fading n=15
- sleep education control n=14
- sleep latency improved in both behavioral groups
- graduated extinction improved awakenings / wake after sleep onset
- salivary cortisol showed small-to-moderate declines, not increases, relative to control
- no significant 12-month group differences in emotional / behavioral problems or secure-insecure attachment

Interpretation:
- does not support a claim that these methods necessarily increase physiological stress
- sample is too small to establish broad safety across all methods

### 6. Longer-term outcomes

Price et al. 2012 was rechecked against PubMed.

Verified:
- 326 children in the original sleep-problem subgroup
- behavioral techniques delivered at 8-10 months
- 225 families (69%) participated at age 6
- no evidence of intervention-control differences in measured child emotional / conduct outcomes, chronic stress, parent-child relationship measures, disinhibited attachment, or maternal mental health

Interpretation:
- reassuring against a large long-term harm effect for this program
- attrition and intervention specificity mean "no detected harm" must not be presented as universal proof of safety

### 7. Infants younger than 6 months — conflicting review evidence

Douglas & Hill 2013 was rechecked against PubMed.

Verified:
- systematic review focused on behavioral sleep interventions in the first 6 months
- did not establish improvement in the key maternal / infant outcomes reviewed
- raised concerns about feeding, crying, maternal anxiety and unsafe separate-room sleeping when such separation is part of an intervention

Interpretation:
- supports maintaining an age-specific evidence boundary
- because the review is older and has a strong interpretive position, it should not be the sole basis for a harm claim

Crichton & Symon 2016 was also independently checked.

Verified:
- review focused on infants under 6 months
- 11 studies identified
- 8 reported improved infant sleep after educational behavioral programs
- authors reached a more favorable conclusion than Douglas & Hill

Interpretation:
- evidence under 6 months is not accurately represented as one-sided
- later Reuter 2020 still identifies <6 months as a knowledge gap
- final claim should be mixed / insufficient for extension, not “proven ineffective”

### 8. Safe sleep boundary — Japan

Current Children and Families Agency guidance was rechecked.

Verified:
- for infants under 1 year, put the infant to sleep supine
- the agency states SIDS incidence is lower when infants are placed supine than prone
- guidance is about SIDS / sleep safety, not efficacy of sleep training

### 9. Safe sleep boundary — AAP

AAP 2022 policy was rechecked.

Verified:
- supine positioning
- firm, flat, noninclined sleep surface
- room sharing without bed sharing
- avoidance of soft bedding and overheating
- recommendations generally apply through the first year

Interpretation:
- sleep-training efficacy must not be used to override safe-sleep guidance

## Interpretation check

PASS WITH LIMITATIONS:
- "sleep training" is decomposed rather than treated as one intervention
- short-term sleep outcomes are separated from total sleep duration
- under-6-month evidence is kept separate
- cortisol evidence is described as one small RCT, not universal proof
- long-term "no harm" wording is nonabsolute
- Japan safe-sleep guidance is separated from overseas efficacy evidence
- no claim is made that one method is best

- Japan-specific efficacy evidence is limited.
- The intervention literature is heterogeneous.
