# Q012 Search Strategy

Last searched: 2026-09-26

## Review type

Targeted evidence review. This is **not** a de novo systematic review.

## Primary question

Does starting English at a younger age itself produce higher later proficiency independently of:
- cumulative hours
- duration
- intensity
- input quality
- interaction
- learning context
- continued use?

## Search streams

1. early-childhood foreign-language ECEC systematic reviews
2. infant/toddler intensive intervention studies
3. social-interaction vs passive-input experiments
4. Japanese age-of-first-exposure / total-hours studies
5. Japanese long-term early-exposure follow-up
6. Japanese earlier-vs-later school-start comparisons
7. instructed-FL age-of-onset literature
8. critical/sensitive-period research
9. pronunciation / phonology domain-specific evidence
10. 2025–2026 updates

## Fresh update — 2026-09-26

Rechecked:
- Thieme et al. 2022 full systematic review
- Ferjan Ramírez & Kuhl 2017
- Kuhl et al. 2003
- Ojima et al. 2011
- Katagiri 2025
- Hartshorne et al. 2018

No new 2026 direct randomized start-age study was identified in this targeted pass that isolates starting age from cumulative exposure in Japanese-primary children.

Recent classroom-age literature located in the update pass does not overturn the current distinction between naturalistic/immersion age effects and low-intensity foreign-language instruction.

## Extraction rules

Where possible, extract separately:
- age of onset
- cumulative hours
- total duration
- weekly intensity
- learning context
- interactive quality
- comparator
- outcome domain
- short-term vs long-term outcome

Classify context:
- ECEC
- extracurricular
- classroom
- immersion
- naturalistic/community
- passive media

Classify outcome:
- phonetic/pronunciation
- vocabulary
- grammar
- listening
- reading
- speaking
- general proficiency

## Interpretation rules

Do not convert:
- early programme success → pure age effect
- longer cumulative exposure → earlier-start causal effect
- short-term vocabulary/phonetic gain → later general proficiency
- immersion evidence → ordinary Japanese low-intensity classroom effect
- pronunciation age sensitivity → all-domain sensitive period
- observational later-start coefficient → recommendation to delay
- critical-period research → preschool deadline
- passive video/audio → interactive exposure

## Limitations

- no database-native export
- no dual screening
- heterogeneous definitions of early exposure
- family self-selection is substantial in long-term observational studies
- Japanese direct randomized start-age evidence is limited
- pronunciation and general proficiency have different age sensitivities
