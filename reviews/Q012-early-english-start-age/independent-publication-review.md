# Q012 Independent Publication Review

Date: 2026-09-26  
Mode: independent reconstruction from major sources; existing Review conclusions were not assumed correct  
Verdict: PASS_WITH_CHANGES  
Final state after required changes: REVIEW

## Independent bottom line

A defensible parent-facing conclusion is:

> 幼児でも、十分な量の対話的で質の高い英語に触れれば英語を学ぶことはできる。ただし、開始年齢が早いこと自体が、累積接触時間、学習環境、指導の質、継続期間とは独立して、後の総合的な英語力を必ず高くするという単純な証拠はない。日本の児童では総接触時間が英語力と強く関連する研究があり、early exposure historyとlater proficiencyのassociationもある。一方、pronunciation / phonologyはgeneral proficiencyよりage-sensitiveな可能性があるため、outcome domainを分けて考える必要がある。

This supports early learnability and sustained-exposure claims, not a universal “start as early as possible” rule.

## Claim reconstruction

### C001 — preschool children can learn English

Decision: SUPPORTED

Thieme 2022:
- foreign-language ECEC generally fostered foreign-language development
- programme quality / input quantity / teacher strategies mattered

Ferjan Ramírez & Kuhl 2017:
- N=126
- age 7–33.5 months
- intensive daily social/play-based English produced measurable gains retained at follow-up

Interpretation:
Young children can learn English under suitable conditions.

Boundary:
Neither source isolates pure age of onset from programme dose / quality.

### C002 — earlier starting age itself causes higher later general proficiency

Decision: NOT ESTABLISHED AS A SIMPLE CAUSAL RULE

The main evidence base repeatedly mixes:
- earlier start
- more cumulative exposure
- longer duration
- different educational context
- family self-selection
- continued later use

Ojima 2011 and instructed-FL literature directly challenge a simplistic age-only interpretation.

### C003 — cumulative exposure matters

Decision: SUPPORTED

Ojima 2011:
- longer total exposure predicted higher English proficiency whether or not first-exposure age was controlled

Thieme 2022:
- prolonged / frequent exposure is an important programme characteristic

Katagiri 2025:
- persistent later differences are compatible with accumulated exposure history

Interpretation:
The quantity/duration of meaningful exposure is a central explanatory variable.

### C004 — quality / interaction matter

Decision: SUPPORTED

Kuhl 2003:
- live social Mandarin exposure supported infant phonetic learning
- matched recorded exposure did not show the same learning effect

Ferjan Ramírez & Kuhl 2017:
- intensive social/play-based programme produced measurable learning

Interpretation:
Exposure mode matters.
Passive media is not interchangeable with high-quality interactive exposure.

### C005 — low-intensity classroom early start automatically creates long-term advantage

Decision: NOT SUPPORTED

The instructed-FL literature shows:
- older starters often learn faster initially
- long-term early-start advantages are often weak/absent in ordinary low-intensity school contexts
- context and cumulative instruction are major moderators

One Japanese private-school comparison also failed to show a clear earlier-start proficiency advantage.

### C006 — early programmes can create short-term English gains

Decision: SUPPORTED WITH PROGRAM BOUNDARIES

Direct programme studies support measurable short-term language/phonetic gains.

These gains must not be automatically re-labelled:
- long-term attainment
- general proficiency
- pure age-of-onset effects

### C007 — Japanese long-term advantage from early English

Decision: ASSOCIATION POSSIBLE / CAUSAL EFFECT NOT ISOLATED

Katagiri 2025:
- 252 Japanese students
- early-exposure groups with 2+ years had higher estimated listening/reading entrance levels
- slope differences across high school were small

Boundary:
Exposure history, cumulative lifetime English, family resources and self-selection remain confounded.

### C008 — preschool is a hard deadline

Decision: NOT SUPPORTED

Hartshorne 2018:
- large grammar-learning dataset
- high learning ability persisted far beyond preschool
- model decline around late adolescence

Interpretation:
This does not prove “all ages are equal,” but it directly undermines a preschool hard-deadline claim.

### C009 — pronunciation / phonology may be more age-sensitive

Decision: SUPPORTED AS A DOMAIN-SPECIFIC BOUNDARY

Infant phonetic-learning and naturalistic pronunciation literature are compatible with stronger age sensitivity for speech/phonology.

This cannot be generalized to:
- reading
- vocabulary
- grammar
- listening
- general classroom English proficiency

## Required challenge 1

Question:
**Would the conclusion change if all studies with unequal cumulative exposure were excluded from the starting-age interpretation?**

Decision:
**NO — the “earlier is always better” claim becomes even weaker.**

Why:
- much of the apparent early-start advantage depends on accumulated years/hours
- Ojima 2011 directly separates first-exposure age from total hours in regression and still shows total hours as the more consistent predictor
- instructed-FL comparisons often show no simple long-term early advantage when exposure is better matched

The core conclusion survives:
starting age alone is not isolated as a universal causal driver of later general proficiency.

## Required challenge 2

Question:
**Is any programme effect being mislabeled as an age effect?**

Decision:
**NO after current corrections.**

Ferjan Ramírez & Kuhl 2017 is explicitly framed as:
- early learnability
- intensive programme efficacy

not:
- proof that starting younger causes superior long-term proficiency

Thieme 2022 is likewise used for ECEC programme effectiveness, not pure age causality.

## Required challenge 3

Question:
**Is any short-term vocabulary or phonetic gain being generalized to long-term general proficiency?**

Decision:
**NO.**

Kuhl 2003 remains a phonetic-learning study.
Madrid 2017 remains a short-term/retained programme-learning study.
Neither is used as direct proof of later general proficiency.

## Required challenge 4

Question:
**Does the Japan-facing conclusion survive without Katagiri 2025?**

Decision:
**YES, but the evidence for persistent later association weakens.**

Without Katagiri:
- Ojima 2011 still supports the importance of exposure hours
- Kurokawa 2020 still challenges a universal earlier-start advantage
- instructed-FL evidence still supports context-dependent age effects

Katagiri adds a useful possible long-term association, but the main conclusion does not depend on it.

## Required challenge 5

Question:
**Is pronunciation evidence being generalized beyond pronunciation / phonology?**

Decision:
**NO.**

The current Reader Layer keeps pronunciation / phonology as a separate domain-specific boundary.

## Required challenge 6

Question:
**Could a reader incorrectly conclude either “preschool is mandatory” or “early English never helps”?**

Decision:
**The current wording avoids both errors.**

It explicitly states:
- young children can learn English
- early high-quality exposure can create real gains
- accumulated experience may be useful
- preschool is not a hard deadline
- starting age alone is not a universal causal rule

## Required changes

1. Keep programme-level early learning evidence separate from pure age-of-onset evidence.
2. Keep total exposure visible whenever first-exposure age is discussed.
3. Keep Katagiri 2025 as association, not starting-age causality.
4. Keep pronunciation / phonology separate from general proficiency.
5. Keep passive media outside the interactive-exposure evidence.
6. Avoid both opposite overstatements: “start as early as possible” and “early English never helps.”

All six are reflected in the current branch.
