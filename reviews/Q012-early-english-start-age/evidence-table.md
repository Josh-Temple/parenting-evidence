## Key extraction boundaries

### A1 Thieme 2022
- 1,812 initial records
- 32 included articles
- 14 quantitative / 16 qualitative / 2 mixed
- 21 foreign-language-development studies
- prolonged/frequent exposure often linked to stronger lasting learning
- exact optimal exposure dose not established
- input quality / teacher practice / programme design matter
- age findings insufficient / inconsistent

### A2 Ferjan Ramírez & Kuhl 2017
- N=126
- 7–33.5 months
- 18 weeks
- daily 1-hour sessions
- native-speaking tutors
- social/play-based method
- matched current-practice comparison
- gains retained 18 weeks after intervention
- programme efficacy ≠ pure younger-start effect

### J1 Ojima 2011
- N=350
- total hours must remain visible beside age of first exposure
- later-start coefficient after controlling hours is NOT advice to delay
- causal starting-age effect not isolated

### J2 Katagiri 2025
- N=252
- high-school listening/reading measured four times over 3 years
- exposure history retrospective/questionnaire-based
- 2+ year early-exposure groups had higher estimated entry levels
- growth slopes differed little
- persistent association ≠ pure start-age causality

### J3 Kurokawa 2020
- Grade 1 vs Grade 5 starters
- no significant overall proficiency difference
- A2 pass rate not higher in Grade 1 group
- contextual contradictory evidence only

## Domain boundary

Keep separate:
- phonetic discrimination / pronunciation
- vocabulary
- grammar
- reading
- listening
- general proficiency

Age sensitivity in one domain does not establish the same age effect in all domains.

## Media boundary

Live/social exposure and passive audiovisual/audio exposure are not equivalent.

Do not infer passive-media efficacy from interactive programme evidence.
