## 1. Thieme et al. 2022

Source:
- DOI 10.1080/07908318.2021.1984498

Verified:
- Web of Science records through 2020-12-10
- 1,812 initial records
- 32 included articles
- 14 quantitative / 16 qualitative / 2 mixed-methods
- foreign-language ECEC generally supported foreign-language development
- input quantity, programme design and teacher strategies were material moderators
- age effects were insufficiently isolated / sometimes contradictory
- no universal optimal starting age or dose was established

Interpretation:
PASS.
Use as evidence that young children can learn in ECEC and that programme characteristics matter, not as proof that the youngest start creates the highest later proficiency.

## 2. Ferjan Ramírez & Kuhl 2017

Source:
- DOI 10.1111/mbe.12144

Verified:
- N=126
- age 7–33.5 months
- 18 weeks
- daily hour-long group English sessions
- native-speaking tutors
- highly social / play-based curriculum
- matched Current Practice Comparison group
- English comprehension / production gains
- gains retained at 18-week follow-up

Interpretation:
PASS_WITH_PROGRAMME_BOUNDARY.
This is direct early-learning evidence, not an equal-hours randomized comparison of starting ages.

## 3. Kuhl et al. 2003

Sources:
- PMID 12861072
- DOI 10.1073/pnas.1532872100

Verified:
- 9-month-old infants
- 12 live Mandarin sessions, 25 minutes each
- live social exposure supported phonetic learning
- matched audiovisual/audio-only exposure did not show the same learning effect

Interpretation:
PASS.
This supports an interaction-quality / phonetic-learning boundary. It is not evidence for passive-media efficacy or long-term general English proficiency.

## 4. Ojima et al. 2011

Sources:
- PMID 21300112
- DOI 10.1016/j.neures.2011.01.018

Verified:
- N=350 Japanese primary-school children
- predictors included age of first exposure and total hours of exposure
- longer total exposure predicted higher English proficiency whether or not age of first exposure was controlled
- when total hours were controlled, later age of first exposure was associated with higher proficiency / larger N400

Interpretation:
PASS_WITH_CAUSAL_BOUNDARY.
The study highlights the importance of amount of exposure. The later-start coefficient is observational/regression evidence and must not become advice to delay English.

## 5. Katagiri 2025

Source:
- DOI 10.20622/jltajournal.28.0_43

Verified:
- N=252 Japanese high-school students
- TOEIC Bridge listening / reading measured four times across three high-school years
- early-English history determined by questionnaire
- groups with two or more years of early exposure/education had higher estimated entrance-level listening/reading than the unexposed group
- growth slopes were similar / differences small, so the initial gap persisted on average

Interpretation:
PASS_WITH_CONFOUNDING_BOUNDARY.
This supports a possible long-term association, not a pure starting-age causal effect. Lifetime cumulative exposure, self-selection, family resources and continued learning remain potential explanations.

## 6. Kurokawa 2020

Source:
- DOI 10.15083/00079279

Verified from the reported study:
- Grade 1 vs Grade 5 starter comparison in one private-school context
- no significant overall English-proficiency advantage for the earlier group
- A2-level pass rate was not higher in the earlier group

Interpretation:
PASS as contradictory/contextual evidence only.
Do not generalize one school nationally.

## 7. Instructed-FL literature

Rechecked across the Muñoz / Pfenninger / Jaekel line:
- older starters often make faster initial progress in classroom settings
- ordinary low-intensity early starts do not consistently create clear long-term superiority
- cumulative exposure, literacy, cognition and later continued use materially shape outcomes

Interpretation:
PASS as context evidence.
Do not transfer naturalistic/immersion critical-period findings directly to low-intensity classroom English.

## 8. Hartshorne et al. 2018

Sources:
- PMID 29729947
- DOI 10.1016/j.cognition.2018.04.007

Verified:
- N=669,498
- grammar-learning ability modeled separately from current age / years of experience
- estimated high learning ability persisted far beyond preschool, with decline beginning around late adolescence (17.4 years in the model)

Interpretation:
PASS_WITH_SCOPE_BOUNDARY.
This study does not establish a preschool deadline, but it is also not a preschool English-program trial or ordinary Japanese EFL study.

## 9. Pronunciation / phonology

Interpretation:
PASS AS DOMAIN-SPECIFIC BOUNDARY.
Earlier age can matter more for phonetic/pronunciation outcomes in some contexts. This must not be generalized to grammar, vocabulary, reading, listening or overall school proficiency.

## 10. Passive-media boundary

Interpretation:
PASS.
Live/social interaction evidence is not treated as equivalent to passive video/audio exposure.
